# Plan how Contexto guidance changes search

The strongest analysis would ask **whether guidance changes the search process, and whether those changes help the model approach and identify the hidden word**. This transfers the useful structure of Word Superiority: estimate the main effect, examine item differences, check systematic errors and repeat consistency, then compare humans and AI under matched conditions. Contexto needs sequential, target-paired analyses rather than a letter-accuracy analysis. The existing study has 25 targets, two instruction conditions and three repetitions, with 30 attempts per game. That supports a focused exploratory study; it does not support treating 150 games as 150 independent puzzles. **This document plans future analysis only: no analysis implementation or new data collection is included.**

**The question must match the manipulation.** The guided condition adds advice to explore different categories, focus on promising clues, and switch after stagnation. It does not record a self-generated plan before play. The saved strategy descriptions are retrospective postgame reports. Therefore, the current question is about supplied guidance, with its three components bundled together. A separate experiment would be needed to test whether generating one's own plan helps. The current protocol and reported outcomes are described in the [Contexto README](../../README.md); the analogy comes from the Word Superiority project state (separate course project).

**Published results motivate separating approach from solving.** In a controlled Contexto adaptation, Hurwitz's 2024 dissertation tested 61 people on nine targets with ten guesses and supplied starting clues around ranks 10, 100 or 1,000. Solve rates were 61%, 22% and 8%, respectively. Consecutive guesses also became more semantically similar when the starting clue was closer. This suggests examining search behavior at different feedback states, but these seeded, short-budget human results cannot serve as an expected solve rate for unseeded live Contexto. The study used its own GloVe scoring environment, rather than the present website. ([Hurwitz, chapter 2, printed pp. 31–42](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4147574w/qt4147574w.pdf))

The closest LLM benchmark, WordPolo, allowed 100 guesses. On 50 shared puzzles, GPT-4.1 solved 42% and o4-mini 62%; three individual humans scored 48%, 72% and 100%. Each human skill level was represented by one person. These are useful demonstrations of attainable performance in that environment, not population human norms or matched benchmarks for this study. No aggregate 30-attempt result was located. Different scoring, vocabulary, targets and budgets prevent a direct leaderboard comparison. ([WordPolo, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19006v1#S5.T1))

**First establish the outcome pattern without changing the primary endpoint.** The existing frozen primary measure compares best-so-far ranks between conditions at each attempt, averages the nine cross-condition repetition pairs within each target, and then weights targets equally. Keep that measure and its whole-target bootstrap procedure. Its already reported score is 0.566, with a 95% interval of 0.492–0.636; 0.5 means no ordinal advantage. This is not a 56.6% solve probability. The interval includes no advantage. Existing summaries also show better median final rank under guidance but fewer exact solutions, which motivates the secondary questions below rather than establishing their explanation. Preserve target pairing: repeated generations reduce one source of variability but do not replace independent targets. ([Existing Contexto results](../../README.md), [Miller, *Adding Error Bars to Evals*](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00640v1))

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does guidance improve search overall? | Present the frozen ordinal trajectory score and interval, alongside cumulative solve curves. | Existing results and trajectories; retain all attempts, including invalid outputs. |
| 2 | Does it help reach a promising neighborhood, find the exact answer, or both? | Cumulative first attainment of ranks ≤100 and ≤10, plus rank 1, across attempts 1–30. Show final-rank distributions. | Existing trajectories; proposed thresholds are exploratory, ordinal milestones rather than equal semantic distances. |
| 3 | Which targets benefit, and how repeatable are the effects? | Target-level guidance scores with all three runs per condition visible; within-target variability and leave-one-target-out sensitivity. | Existing target scores and trajectories; distinguishes target difficulty from lucky generations. |
| 4 | Does observed behavior follow the advice? | Early category breadth, relation to the current best clue, switches after stalls; coded guess timelines. | Requires new semantic annotation or a validated, fixed embedding method. |
| 5 | Are systematic mistakes consuming the budget? | Invalid attempts, repeated words/lemmas, stereotyped openings and revisits; error-category bars and representative timelines. | Existing logs, sometimes browser evidence; retain the original budget accounting. |
| 6 | Does effort vary with search difficulty? | Thought-token distributions by search state, stall and subsequent improvement; separate input-token growth and latency. | Existing event logs; associations within AI, not direct cognitive equivalence to human reaction time. |
| 7 | Do humans respond to guidance similarly? | Matched human–AI outcome and strategy comparisons, target difficulty agreement, guidance-by-agent contrast. | Future human collection with balanced targets, participant-aware uncertainty and stable feedback. |

For priorities 1–3, use **paired whole-target uncertainty**, keeping each target's two conditions and repetitions together. The nine repetition pairs are dependent, as are adjacent attempts. Avoid independent-trial tests on all guess rows. With 25 targets, emphasize estimates and intervals rather than many significance tests. Label newly chosen endpoints exploratory: writing this plan after inspecting existing summaries does not make it a preregistration.

Efficiency should include failures. Plan cumulative solve curves and a clearly defined 30-attempt restricted time-to-solve summary; mark unsolved games as administratively censored at the budget. A mean calculated only among solved games compares different selected subsets. Similarly, studying what happens after reaching the top 10 is descriptive: guidance itself can change which games enter that subgroup. It cannot identify a causal effect on “finishing ability.”

**Then test observable search behavior.** Hills, Jones and Todd studied 141 people generating animal names. A dynamic model using local and global retrieval cues fit 131 participants better than a static model. This motivates separate measures for local exploitation and transitions between semantic regions. However, their task involved freely retrieving category members, with no hidden target or feedback. It does not establish that more switching improves Contexto. ([Hills et al., 2012](https://willamannering.weebly.com/uploads/1/2/1/7/121718304/hillsetalpsychrev2012__1_.pdf))

Before coding, define an early window, such as the first five attempts, and a transparent category scheme. Measure category coverage rather than calling unique word counts “semantic diversity.” Define exploitation using the relationship between a new guess and the best clue available at that moment. Define stagnation as consecutive attempts without a new best rank, and declare a small set of stall thresholds because the instructions say only “several.” Measure whether a semantic switch follows a stall and whether later progress follows that switch. Difficult games can cause both switching and poor outcomes, so these associations cannot establish a mechanism.

Start with a balanced manual coding pilot, blind to condition and outcome where practical, and have another coder check a subset. Keep an ambiguous category instead of forcing polysemous words into a single interpretation. If automation becomes useful, select and freeze an embedding model, validate its distances against annotations, and obtain approval before adding dependencies. Independent embeddings describe a proxy semantic space; they do not reveal the live game's scoring geometry. Use two or three timelines selected by a declared rule to illustrate patterns after the full summaries are known. An attractive two-dimensional embedding map should remain illustrative.

Recent fluency evidence makes this a plausible question rather than a guaranteed finding. A preliminary 2026 study reported category-switch ratios around 0.55 for humans versus 0.40 for Llama models, despite correlated category-transition patterns. Its seeded animal-fluency sequences differ from Contexto, and it does not test this Gemini model. ([Lacosse et al., preliminary preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.01822v1))

Error checks provide the closest equivalent to Word Superiority's response-bias analysis. Separate fixed opening words from full-sequence semantic breadth; distinguish exact repeats, website-recognized lemma repeats, rejected vocabulary and malformed outputs. Report budget lost to these errors. A valid-guesses-only sensitivity view can describe inefficiency, but excluding errors changes the treatment comparison. Compare postgame claims about exploration or switching with coded sequences as self-report agreement, not internal reasoning. Generated explanations can omit causes of model answers. ([Turpin et al., 2023](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2023/hash/ed3fea9033a80fea1376299fa7863f4a-Abstract-Conference.html))

**Keep the human comparison matched and modest.** The planned ten humans, five per instruction condition and five games each, can cover each of the 25 targets once per condition. Balance assignment and game order, avoid repeating a target within a person, and match instructions, available history, feedback and the 30-attempt cap. Record experience, English proficiency, prior puzzle exposure, guesses, ranks, invalids and decision timestamps. Use supplied advice for both agent types if the aim is to compare this manipulation; a human-generated plan would test a different intervention.

There are only five independent people per arm. Account for participant and target dependence, and describe the guidance-by-agent interaction with substantial uncertainty. Similar average performance would not establish similar mechanisms. A more informative comparison includes which targets are difficult and how breadth, local refinement and switching differ. Human decision intervals and AI token usage can each describe effort within an agent type; API latency includes service and network factors and should not be treated as human reaction time.

Before collection, define feedback-stability checks against archived sentinel ranks. These checks detect some drift but cannot certify the entire live scorer. Material discrepancies should trigger a documented comparability decision. A frozen scorer or matched starting clues would be valuable future experiments, declared separately from the completed live study. The earlier 70-attempt pilot should remain separate.

**The practical deliverable should be a small analysis package with a clear claim.** An achievable contribution is to establish the uncertainty in guidance effects, determine whether approach and exact solving tell different stories, and document observable strategy changes and target dependence. Significant improvement is not required for a useful course result. Do not pursue a broad theory of human–AI semantic cognition from 25 targets and a small human sample.
